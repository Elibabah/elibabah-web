## Decision one: a pure, deterministic session engine (ADR-0006)

{/*
  DEVELOP: Your flagship decision. Give it the most room.
  - The before: exam logic lived in a ~350-line hook, leaning on useRef to dodge
    stale closures and on module-level singletons. Slow to test (jest-expo renders
    hooks; the suite took ~10 minutes) and fragile on state reset.
  - The decision: extract the logic into a pure engine (src/engine/) with no React,
    Expo, globals, or clocks. Plain data in (GPS fixes, injected timestamps, voice
    exchanges), COMMANDS out. useDrivingSession.ts becomes a thin adapter that
    executes them, and is the only layer that touches the outside world.
  - The detail that makes the boundary concrete, use it, an abstract claim about
    purity persuades nobody: a speak command carries a priority, so safety and
    navigation interrupt whatever is being said while coaching is dropped if TTS is
    already playing; a requestReroute is fetched by the adapter and handed back
    through applyReroute, which keeps the network out of the engine entirely. The
    engine decides, the adapter acts.
  - The payoff (the trade-off accepted): a large refactor cost, but it unlocks
    deterministic replay, a real GPS track plus a transcript replayed in a unit
    test in milliseconds, and it makes the later Android port (MVP-5) feasible by
    reusing the engine intact. The suite now runs in ~4s, down from ~10min.
  - Why this is the decision that best demonstrates judgement: it trades short-term
    effort for testability, portability, and confidence. That is engineering, not
    output.
*/}

## Decision two: real NZTA scoring, not an invented percentage (ADR-0005)

{/*
  DEVELOP: Your product-judgement decision, not just technical.
  - The choice: align scoring to official NZTA categories (critical errors,
    immediate-fail conditions) rather than a percentage score invented for effect.
  - Why it matters: it answers the user's real question, "would I have passed",
    instead of a number that looks precise but means nothing.
  - The shape of the decision, now that it has shipped (engine/nztaVerdict.ts,
    2026-08-04): fail = any immediate-fail error, or more than one critical. The
    speed monitor was realigned to the official thresholds, >=10 km/h over at any
    duration, or >=5 km/h over sustained for >=5 s, as immediate fail; a brief 5-10
    over as critical, reported at incident end. The old numeric score survives, but
    demoted to a secondary trend metric rather than the verdict.
  - The trade-off accepted: it requires maintaining a sourced event-to-category
    mapping table by hand (docs/nzta-error-mapping.md), kept current against the
    assessment guide. That is ongoing manual work, not a one-off.
  - The honest boundary, and the part worth dwelling on: some official categories
    are simply unobservable from GPS and voice, mirror checks, head checks,
    signalling, lane position, vehicle control. So the verdict states what it did
    NOT assess. A partial assessment that declares its own limits is more useful
    than a complete-looking one that quietly guesses.
  - CAVEAT to flag: alignment to NZTA categories is a design intent, not a claim of
    official equivalence or endorsement. Keep the wording careful.
*/}

## Decision three: getting lost is not a fail (deviation evaluation)

{/*
  DEVELOP: Your domain-fidelity decision. This one is about reading the rules of the
  real test properly, not about code, and it is the freshest material you have
  (shipped 2026-08-02, engine/__tests__/deviation.test.ts).
  - The insight that drives it: on the real NZTA test, getting lost is not an error;
    disobeying signs and markings is. The naive build punishes every wrong turn. That
    is a simulation of a driving test, not a simulation of driving.
  - The before: the app scolded the driver immediately on going off route, which is
    both wrong against the rubric and hostile at exactly the moment a driver is most
    loaded.
  - The decision, in three beats (a natural triad, use it): deviation past 300 m
    triggers a SILENT reroute with no reprimand; once the new route is applied the
    engine emits askDeviation; only then does Sam ask why, and Claude classifies the
    spoken answer as justified (closure, obstruction, safety, no penalty plus a
    positive judgement note) or manoeuvring error (mild penalty retained).
  - The trade-off accepted: a round trip to the model inside a live drive, and a
    classification that can be wrong. The mitigation is the fallback, if the AI is
    unreachable the result is manoeuvring_error, which is exactly the pre-existing
    behaviour, so an offline session is never worse off than before the feature
    existed. Name that principle explicitly: a new dependency should degrade to the
    old behaviour, not to a broken one.
  - The wider point to land: this is where domain research shows up as engineering.
    The interesting work was reading the assessment criteria closely enough to know
    that the obvious penalty was the wrong one.
*/}

## Decision four: the honest gap, hands-free audio (ADR-0003)

{/*
  DEVELOP: The decision that shows honesty and risk-thinking. Do not hide it.
  - The core product constraint: a truly hands-free session, phone untouched for
    the full twenty minutes. This is the vision.
  - The reality: the original voice library crashed on an AVAudioPCMBuffer
    exception and was replaced by expo-speech-recognition, but true always-listening
    is unresolved. Today it is tap-to-speak, which contradicts the central
    constraint. This is the largest gap between product vision and current code.
  - The framing: name it as an open spike (full-duplex vs half-duplex), a known
    unknown being worked deliberately, not a failure swept aside. MVP-2 is blocked
    on this spike, and saying so is a strength, not an apology.
*/}

## Other decisions worth noting

{/*
  DEVELOP (briefly, one short paragraph each or a tight prose cluster):
  - ADR-0001: all AI calls go through a Supabase Edge Function proxy. One extra
    network hop (tens of ms) in exchange for never shipping API keys in the client
    bundle, a blocker for TestFlight distribution.
  - ADR-0002: guest tier fully ephemeral, nothing persisted. Loses local-to-account
    progress migration, but avoids orphaned data and gives the simplest privacy
    story.
  - ADR-0004: OSM/Overpass as the source of speed limits and checkpoints, replacing
    a hardcoded 50 km/h limit and a sign-detection path that never fired. Shipped
    2026-07-23: a corridor is prefetched per route (maxspeed zones, stop signs,
    signals, give-way, level and pedestrian crossings) and evaluated by GPS
    proximity. Costs an extra network call and imperfect NZ data coverage, with a
    fallback to the old heuristics when Overpass is down. Worth one line on the
    field bug that motivated it: signals now suppress the unexpected-stop and
    harsh-braking nudges, because queueing at a red light was being recorded as a
    fault.
  - Destination validation (2026-08-04): one Overpass query checks all 8 candidate
    bearings for urban drivable streets and snaps the destination onto the nearest
    one. The elegant part, and the reason it is worth a sentence: sea bearings are
    not special-cased, they simply have no streets, so they are never picked.
  - Positioning: MapView.onUserLocationChange as the authoritative source (not
    followsUserLocation, unreliable with PROVIDER_GOOGLE); watchPositionAsync as
    fallback.
  - Dynamic rerouteing: no fixed route; recalculated from current GPS whenever a
    leg completes or the driver strays >300m, with a 20s debounce.
*/}

## The honest state of the work

{/*
  DEVELOP: This section is a strength; frame it as method, not confession.
  - Active technical debt: true hands-free not yet implemented (the biggest gap,
    tap-to-speak today); uneven OSM coverage in NZ, so the app falls back to
    instruction-text heuristics and a 50 km/h default when Overpass is thin or down;
    simulate_drive.sh referenced in CLAUDE.md but absent from the repo, its GPS
    replayer replacement scheduled for MVP-4.
  - The distinction worth drawing, and the strongest thing in this section: MVP-1 is
    code complete but not field complete. Two exit criteria are still open because
    both need a car rather than a simulator, that a replayed real-drive GPS track
    produces identical event streams across runs, and that a mapped stop sign taken
    at 15 km/h is recorded as a violation while a genuine full stop is recorded as
    compliant. Everything provable in a unit test has been proven. Naming the
    boundary between "tested" and "validated" is the point; a lot of software ships
    without anyone drawing that line.
  - Roadmap status (per docs/ROADMAP.md, progress through August 2026): MVP-0
    complete (AI proxy in production with no extractable key, schema v2 with 60s
    checkpointing verified on device, false reroutes fixed, CI green with typecheck
    + 254 tests across 16 suites in ~4s, down from ~10min). MVP-1 code complete
    (pure engine, real OSM data, deviation classification, NZTA verdict, destination
    validation) with two field exit criteria open. MVP-2 next, blocked on the
    ADR-0003 spike. MVP-3 (user tiers), MVP-4 (product quality, E2E, TestFlight) and
    MVP-5 planned.
  - The argument to make: documenting debt precisely is itself an engineering
    practice. Knowing exactly what is not done, and why, is a form of control.
*/}
