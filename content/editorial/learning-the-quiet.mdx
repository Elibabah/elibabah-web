The first winter in the South Island taught me to listen before it taught me anything else. I had arrived expecting scenery, the postcard of mountains, and I found instead a silence that asks something of you; a silence that is not empty but full, the way a held breath is full. Put another way: the landscape here does not perform. It waits.

I keep returning to that first morning. Frost on the glass, the light coming up slow and grey over the hills, and no sound at all except my own uncertainty. Nobody tells you that moving to the far edge of the world is less an arrival than a kind of undressing; you leave behind your language, your weather, the face the mirror expects of you, and you stand in the cold as someone not yet finished. And yet the cold is honest. It does not pretend you belong before you do.

What is home, if not the place that agrees to wait while you become someone who can live in it? I am still learning the answer. The mountains, at least, are patient teachers.
