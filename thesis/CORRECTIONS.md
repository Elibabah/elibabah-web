# Corrections noted while translating

**Not published.** Kept out of the English PDF at Elías's instruction: the published edition
follows the original. This file is the working record, should the Spanish edition ever be
revised.

Two classes of thing are recorded below. The orthographic slips in the bibliography **were**
silently corrected in the English edition, as ordinary translation hygiene. The substantive
items were **not** acted on; the English follows the Spanish as printed.

## Bibliography

The following were corrected silently in this edition and should also be corrected in the Spanish
one. All are orthographic slips in names, except the first, which is a misattribution.

**Substantive**

- **SARTRE, Jean-Paul → Jean Paul.** *"Discurso de Cristo muerto desde lo alto del Cosmos, diciendo
  que no hay Dios"* is by **Jean Paul**, the pen name of Johann Paul Friedrich Richter, from
  *Siebenkäs* (1796). It is not by Sartre. The entry should move to **JEAN PAUL** in the alphabetical
  sequence; it has been left in place here, marked, rather than reordering the bibliography
  unilaterally. See also Ch. 3, notes 115 and 122.

**Alphabetical order.** Two entries sit out of sequence in the original and have been left where they
are: ESQUILO (Aeschylus) appears after ELIADE, and the three S/A (anonymous) entries appear between
SUBIRATS and TODOROV. Under the Spanish alphabetisation both are defensible; under English
alphabetisation, AESCHYLUS would move up and ANON. would move to the A's. Worth a decision before
typesetting.

## Substantive items found in the body, not acted on

- **Ch. 3, notes 115 and 122** — *"Discurso de Cristo muerto desde lo alto del Cosmos, diciendo
  que no hay Dios"* is attributed to Jean-Paul Sartre. The text is by **Jean Paul**, pen name of
  Johann Paul Friedrich Richter, from *Siebenkäs* (1796).
- **Ch. 3, note 84** — the line *"aquí está todavía el olor de la sangre"* is cited to *Hamlet*.
  It is Lady Macbeth's, *Macbeth* V.i.
- **Ch. 2 and Ch. 3** — *Creonte* (Creon) appears where *Caronte* (Charon) is meant, the ferryman
  being correctly named elsewhere in Ch. 2.
- **Conclusions** — the single footnote is numbered 343, exactly one more than the total of all
  preceding notes (54 + 54 + 76 + 62 + 96 = 342): an automatic number never reset when the
  chapter sequences were restarted by hand.
